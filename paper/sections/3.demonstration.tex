Figure~\ref{fig:demo_ui} shows a live session. Attendees can try three
interaction scenarios.

\subsubsection{Regional dependence.}
The attendee uploads an image, marks a visual cue such as a sign,
licence plate or uniform, and suppresses attention towards its region. A large map
displacement shows sensitivity to this attention intervention; a small one
indicates a prediction that is stable under the chosen configuration.
Suppressing an equal background rectangle shows whether the response is
specific to the cue; neither establishes that the cue explains the prediction.

\subsubsection{Proposal comparison.}
For a benchmark example, cached WeDetect-Uni proposals can be applied
individually or as a union. The resulting displacements distinguish sensitivity
to one foreground object from sensitivity to the broader object-bearing region,
without a text prompt tailored to the image.

\subsubsection{Layer localisation.}
The attendee compares a single active layer with a multi-layer schedule.
Broader intervention often increases displacement without improving error,
mirroring Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Resetting all biases to zero restores
the baseline exactly.

\subsection{Deployment and Relevance}

Inference runs offline on the demonstration machine we bring; the appendix
states the setup requirements. The implementation, evaluation scripts and
full-dataset run results are at
\url{https://github.com/Le-Anh-Duy/geoprobe}. Observing how attention
interventions move a geographic prediction is relevant to both multimedia
verification and the privacy risks of inferring location from untagged images.
