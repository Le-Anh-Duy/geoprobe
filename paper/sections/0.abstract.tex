\begin{abstract}
Worldwide image geolocation models such as GeoCLIP estimate GPS coordinates
from a single photograph, yet they provide no account of which visual evidence
supports a prediction. Post-hoc saliency highlights regions associated with
a model output, but does not directly measure the output change under an
internal attention intervention. We present GeoProbe, an interactive system for probing image geolocation through controlled attention interventions. Users select a region manually or from WeDetect-Uni proposals and configure per-layer attention biases. GeoProbe applies these biases to the pre-softmax attention logits of the CLIP vision tower. It executes GeoCLIP with and without the
intervention and displays both predictions and their geodesic displacement.
On all $2{,}997$ Img2GPS3k images, we search single and multi-layer
interventions using a discovery split and evaluate the selected configuration
on a held-out split. Scoring by clipped mean improvement over the unmodified
model, so that positive values mean reduced error, a three-layer configuration
achieves $+11.94$~km on discovery but $-3.66$~km on holdout
(95\% CI $[-20.43,12.40]$), and does not significantly outperform the
selected single layer. Nevertheless, it moves the predicted coordinate by
$189.5$~km on average while retaining an image-embedding cosine similarity of
$0.9984$. The results position
GeoProbe as a tool for measuring output sensitivity to controlled attention
interventions rather than as an accuracy-improvement mechanism, and demonstrate
the importance of held-out validation when interpreting attention
interventions.

\keywords{Image Geolocation \and Explainability \and Attention Intervention
\and Vision--Language Models.}
\end{abstract}
